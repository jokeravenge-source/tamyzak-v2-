## Section 1: Biochemistry & Carbohydrates

Flashcards 1–8

Flashcard 1 — Define biochemistry.

Answer: Biochemistry is the branch of science that studies the chemical changes and biosynthesis occurring in living organisms and explains biological phenomena in chemical terms.

Flashcard 2 — Define carbohydrates.

Answer: Carbohydrates are organic compounds consisting of carbon, hydrogen, and oxygen. Their general formula is commonly represented as (CH₂O)ₙ or Cₙ(H₂O)ₙ.

Flashcard 3 — Why are carbohydrates called carbohydrates?

Answer: Because their name is derived from carbon and hydrate (water), reflecting their general composition.

Flashcard 4 — Classify carbohydrates and give examples of each.

Answer:

1. Monosaccharides: Glucose and fructose.
2. Disaccharides: Sucrose, maltose, and lactose.
3. Polysaccharides: Starch and cellulose.

Flashcard 5 — What are the two general groups of carbohydrates?

Answer:

1. Simple carbohydrates.
2. Complex carbohydrates.

Flashcard 6 — In which structural forms can carbohydrates exist?

Answer: Open-chain and cyclic structures.

Flashcard 7 — Give an example of a disaccharide.

Answer: Maltose.

Flashcard 8 — Give two examples of polysaccharides.

Answer: Starch and cellulose.

## Section 2: Glucose

Flashcards 9–19

Flashcard 9 — Define glucose and mention its occurrence in nature.

Answer: Glucose is a monosaccharide known as grape sugar. It is naturally found in grapes and blood.

Flashcard 10 — What is the molecular formula of glucose?

Answer: C₆H₁₂O₆ or C₆(H₂O)₆.

Flashcard 11 — Explain the open and cyclic structures of glucose.

Answer:

- Open structure: Contains one aldehyde group (–CHO) and several hydroxyl groups (–OH).
- Cyclic structure: Contains an ether linkage and several hydroxyl groups.

Flashcard 12 — Justify: Glucose has a high boiling point.

Answer: Because hydrogen bonds form between glucose molecules through their hydroxyl groups, increasing its boiling point.

Flashcard 13 — Justify: Glucose dissolves strongly in water.

Answer: Because glucose forms hydrogen bonds with water molecules.

Flashcard 14 — Justify: Glucose has chemical properties similar to alcohols and aldehydes.

Answer: Because its open structure contains several hydroxyl groups (–OH) and one aldehyde group (–CHO).

Flashcard 15 — Why does glucose react with Tollens' reagent and Fehling's solution?

Answer: Because glucose contains an aldehyde group in its open-chain structure and behaves as a reducing sugar.

Flashcard 16 — Mention the physical properties of glucose.

Answer:

1. Crystalline solid.
2. High boiling point.
3. Soluble in water.
4. Forms hydrogen bonds.

Flashcard 17 — Mention an important medical use of glucose.

Answer: It is used as a nutrient for patients who cannot be fed orally and during surgical operations.

Flashcard 18 — Write the open structural formula of glucose.

Answer:

```
    CHO
     |
   H–C–OH
     |
  HO–C–H
     |
   H–C–OH
     |
   H–C–OH
     |
   CH₂OH
```

Flashcard 19 — What functional group distinguishes the open structure of glucose?

Answer: The aldehyde group (–CHO).

## Section 3: Fructose

Flashcards 20–27

Flashcard 20 — Define fructose.

Answer: Fructose is a monosaccharide known as fruit sugar, found naturally in honey and most fruits.

Flashcard 21 — What is the molecular formula of fructose?

Answer: C₆H₁₂O₆.

Flashcard 22 — Compare glucose and fructose.

Answer:

- Both have the molecular formula C₆H₁₂O₆.
- Glucose contains an aldehyde group in its open structure.
- Fructose contains a ketone group in its open structure.
- Both contain hydroxyl groups.

Flashcard 23 — Explain the open and cyclic structures of fructose.

Answer:

- Open structure: Contains a ketone carbonyl group and several hydroxyl groups.
- Cyclic structure: Contains an ether linkage and hydroxyl groups.

Flashcard 24 — Justify: Fructose is classified as a reducing sugar although it is a ketone.

Answer: Because fructose gives positive reactions with Tollens' reagent and Fehling's solution.

Flashcard 25 — Why do fructose and glucose have similar physical properties?

Answer: Because they have the same molecular formula and contain several hydroxyl groups.

Flashcard 26 — Why is fructose soluble in water and has a high boiling point?

Answer: Because it contains hydroxyl groups capable of forming hydrogen bonds.

Flashcard 27 — What functional group distinguishes open-chain fructose from glucose?

Answer: The ketone group (C=O).

## Section 4: Disaccharides & Sucrose

Flashcards 28–35

Flashcard 28 — Define disaccharides.

Answer: Disaccharides are carbohydrates formed from two monosaccharide units joined by a glycosidic bond with the elimination of one water molecule.

Flashcard 29 — Give three examples of disaccharides.

Answer: Sucrose, maltose, and lactose.

Flashcard 30 — Define sucrose.

Answer: Sucrose is a disaccharide known as cane sugar, produced from sugar cane and consisting of glucose and fructose.

Flashcard 31 — What is the molecular formula of sucrose?

Answer: C₁₂H₂₂O₁₁.

Flashcard 32 — Name the monosaccharides that form sucrose.

Answer: Glucose and fructose.

Flashcard 33 — What type of bond connects glucose and fructose in sucrose?

Answer: A glycosidic bond.

Flashcard 34 — Write the formation equation of sucrose.

Answer:

Glucose + Fructose → Sucrose + H₂O

C₆H₁₂O₆ + C₆H₁₂O₆ → C₁₂H₂₂O₁₁ + H₂O

Flashcard 35 — What happens to sucrose during digestion?

Answer: Its glycosidic bond is broken by hydrolysis, producing glucose and fructose.

Sucrose + H₂O → Glucose + Fructose

## Section 5: Proteins & Amino Acids

Flashcards 36–49

Flashcard 36 — Define proteins.

Answer: Proteins are organic compounds containing carbon, hydrogen, oxygen, and nitrogen, sometimes sulfur and phosphorus. They are formed by the bonding of many amino acids through peptide bonds.

Flashcard 37 — What are the main building units of proteins?

Answer: Amino acids.

Flashcard 38 — What elements are found in proteins?

Answer: Carbon, hydrogen, oxygen, and nitrogen. Some proteins also contain sulfur and phosphorus.

Flashcard 39 — What is the general structural formula of an amino acid?

Answer:

```
      H
      |
H₂N – C – COOH
      |
      R
```

Where:

- –NH₂: Amino group.
- –COOH: Carboxyl group.
- R: Alkyl or side-chain group.

Flashcard 40 — What two functional groups are found in all amino acids?

Answer: Amino group (–NH₂) and carboxyl group (–COOH).

Flashcard 41 — What causes the differences between amino acids?

Answer: Differences in their R groups (side chains).

Flashcard 42 — Justify: Proteins have amphoteric properties.

Answer: Because they contain carboxyl groups with acidic properties and amino groups with basic properties, allowing them to react with both acids and bases.

Flashcard 43 — Why can proteins react with acids and bases?

Answer: Because they possess both acidic carboxyl groups and basic amino groups.

Flashcard 44 — Justify: Proteins cannot easily be separated by simple chemical methods.

Answer: Because they have similar chemical compositions and physical and chemical properties.

Flashcard 45 — How are peptide bonds formed?

Answer: By joining amino acids together with the elimination of water molecules.

Flashcard 46 — What determines the functions of proteins?

Answer: The types, numbers, and sequences of amino acids within their structures.

Flashcard 47 — How can proteins be broken down into amino acids?

Answer: By hydrolysis using inorganic acids such as HCl, which breaks peptide (amide) bonds.

Flashcard 48 — What happens when proteins react with NaOH?

Answer: They can be broken down to form salts of amino acids.

Flashcard 49 — Give examples of important proteins in living organisms.

Answer: Enzymes, hormones, hemoglobin, and keratin.

## Section 6: Enzymes

Ministerial focus

Flashcards 50–61

Flashcard 50 — Define enzymes.

Ministerial: 2013 Preliminary, 2014 First Round, 2016, 2017, 2019 (as indicated in the chapter)

Answer: Enzymes are biological catalysts, generally proteins, produced by living organisms. They participate in vital processes such as digestion, metabolism, and respiration by reducing the energy required for reactions.

Flashcard 51 — Enumerate the properties of enzymes.

Ministerial: 2013, 2014, 2016, 2017

Answer:

1. They are proteins.
2. They are found in living cells.
3. They act as biological catalysts.
4. They participate in digestion, metabolism, and respiration.
5. They are produced in living organisms.
6. They are constantly renewed.
7. They work at a specific pH.
8. High temperatures can destroy their activity.
9. They reduce the energy needed for reactions.

Flashcard 52 — What are the two types of enzymes?

Answer: Internal enzymes and external enzymes.

Flashcard 53 — Define internal enzymes.

Ministerial: 2014 Second Round, 2016 Third Round, 2018 Third Round

Answer: Internal enzymes perform their functions inside cells and do not pass through the cell membrane, such as oxidative enzymes.

Flashcard 54 — Define external enzymes.

Ministerial: 2013 Second Round, 2015 First Round, 2018 First Round

Answer: External enzymes perform their functions outside cells after being secreted, such as digestive enzymes.

Flashcard 55 — Compare internal and external enzymes.

Answer:

| Internal enzymes           | External enzymes           |
| -------------------------- | -------------------------- |
| Work inside cells          | Work outside cells         |
| Do not leave the cell      | Are secreted from cells    |
| Example: Oxidative enzymes | Example: Digestive enzymes |

Flashcard 56 — Justify: High temperatures affect enzyme activity.

Answer: Because high temperatures disrupt the enzyme structure and prevent it from functioning properly.

Flashcard 57 — Why do enzymes work at a specific pH?

Answer: Their activity depends on the surrounding pH, and they function effectively within a suitable pH range.

Flashcard 58 — Why are enzymes continuously renewed in living organisms?

Answer: Because they gradually lose their effectiveness over time.

Flashcard 59 — What is the role of enzymes in biological reactions?

Answer: They act as catalysts that facilitate chemical reactions by lowering the required activation energy.

Flashcard 60 — Fill in the blanks: External enzymes perform their duties \_\_\_\_\_\_ cells, such as \_\_\_\_\_\_ enzymes.

Answer: Outside; digestive.

Flashcard 61 — Give an example of each type of enzyme.

Answer:

- Internal: Oxidative enzymes.
- External: Digestive enzymes.

Exam note: The source's English explanation says enzymes supply energy for reactions, while its Arabic explanation describes reducing the required energy. The flashcards use the latter meaning.

## Section 7: Lipids & Fats

Flashcards 62–71

Flashcard 62 — Define lipids.

Answer: Lipids are organic compounds represented by animal fats and vegetable oils. They are used for energy storage and dissolve in organic solvents rather than water.

Flashcard 63 — Compare animal fats and vegetable oils.

Answer:

| Animal fats                         | Vegetable oils                           |
| ----------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| Generally solid at room temperature | Generally liquid at room temperature     |
| Found in animal tissues             | Found in plants, such as cotton and corn |

Flashcard 64 — Why are lipids important to living organisms?

Answer: They store energy that can be released when needed through digestion and oxidation.

Flashcard 65 — Mention industrial uses of lipids.

Answer: Soap, paint, and candle production.

Flashcard 66 — Why don't lipids dissolve in water?

Answer: Because they are largely nonpolar compounds, while water is polar.

Flashcard 67 — Name two organic solvents in which lipids dissolve.

Answer: Ether and chloroform.

Flashcard 68 — How are triglycerides formed?

Answer: By the esterification of glycerol with three fatty acid molecules.

Flashcard 69 — Write the general equation for triglyceride formation.

Answer:

Glycerol + 3 Fatty acids → Triglyceride + 3H₂O

Flashcard 70 — What is the carbon-chain range of fatty acids mentioned in the chapter?

Answer: C₁₂–C₂₄.

Flashcard 71 — What functional group is found at one end of a fatty acid?

Answer: The carboxyl group (–COOH).

## Section 8: Soaps & Saponification

Ministerial focus

Flashcards 72–83

Flashcard 72 — Define soap.

Ministerial: 2014 First Round

Answer: Soap is the sodium or potassium salt of fatty acids.

Flashcard 73 — Define saponification.

Answer: Saponification is the reaction of fats or oils with strong bases, such as NaOH or KOH, to produce soap and glycerol.

Flashcard 74 — What raw materials are used in soap production?

Answer: Fats or vegetable oils and sodium hydroxide (NaOH) or potassium hydroxide (KOH).

Flashcard 75 — Write the general saponification equation.

Answer:

Triglyceride + 3NaOH → Glycerol + 3RCOONa

Where RCOONa represents the sodium salt of a fatty acid (soap).

Flashcard 76 — Write the equation for the production of sodium palmitate soap.

Ministerial: 2014, 2015 Preliminary

Answer:

Glyceryl tripalmitate + 3NaOH → Glycerol + 3Sodium palmitate

C₃H₅(OCOC₁₅H₃₁)₃ + 3NaOH → C₃H₅(OH)₃ + 3C₁₅H₃₁COONa

Flashcard 77 — On what do the shape and quality of soap depend?

Answer: On the type of base and oil used during saponification.

Flashcard 78 — What type of soap is produced using sodium hydroxide?

Answer: Hard soap, such as ordinary soap bars.

Flashcard 79 — What type of soap is produced using potassium hydroxide?

Answer: Soft soap, such as liquid soap and shaving foam.

Flashcard 80 — Justify: Sodium chloride is added during soap production.

Answer: To separate and precipitate the soap from the reaction mixture.

Flashcard 81 — Why is the final soap product washed with water?

Answer: To remove remaining salt.

Flashcard 82 — Why are calcium hydroxide and magnesium hydroxide not used instead of NaOH and KOH in soap production?

Ministerial: 2017 First Round, Outside Iraq

Answer: Because Ca²⁺ and Mg²⁺ ions are associated with water hardness, and soap does not foam properly in hard water.

Flashcard 83 — Compare the products formed when NaOH and KOH are used in saponification.

Answer:

- NaOH: Sodium salts of fatty acids, producing hard soap.
- KOH: Potassium salts of fatty acids, producing soft soap.

## Section 9: Polysaccharides, Starch & Cellulose

Flashcards 84–95

Flashcard 84 — Define polysaccharides.

Answer: Polysaccharides are large carbohydrate molecules (polymers) formed by joining many monosaccharide units.

Flashcard 85 — Give two examples of polysaccharides.

Answer: Starch and cellulose.

Flashcard 86 — Define starch.

Answer: Starch is a polysaccharide formed by linking many glucose molecules together to produce a large polymer.

Flashcard 87 — What is the basic structural unit of starch?

Answer: Glucose.

Flashcard 88 — Give an example of a food that contains starch.

Answer: Potato.

Flashcard 89 — Define cellulose.

Answer: Cellulose is a polysaccharide consisting of many glucose units linked together to form a large polymer.

Flashcard 90 — What are the natural sources of cellulose mentioned in the chapter?

Answer: Wood fibers and the peels of some fruits, including dates.

Flashcard 91 — Why do starch and cellulose have different properties although both are formed from glucose?

Answer: Because they differ in the number of glucose units and the way these units are bonded together.

Flashcard 92 — What substances can break down cellulose molecules according to the chapter?

Answer: Certain enzymes and acidic solutions.

Flashcard 93 — How can starch be detected?

Answer: Add a few drops of iodine solution in potassium iodide to the sample. The appearance of a blue color indicates starch.

Flashcard 94 — How can you distinguish between starch and glucose?

Answer:

- Starch: Produces a blue color with iodine solution.
- Glucose: Does not produce the blue color with iodine solution.

Flashcard 95 — What is the basic unit common to both starch and cellulose?

Answer: Glucose.

## Section 10: Important Protein Reactions & Chapter Exercises

Flashcards 96–106

Flashcard 96 — What happens when proteins undergo hydrolysis?

Answer: Their peptide bonds break, producing smaller units and ultimately amino acids.

Flashcard 97 — What is the effect of HCl on proteins?

Answer: It breaks the amide (peptide) bonds during hydrolysis, producing amino acids.

Flashcard 98 — What is the effect of NaOH on proteins?

Answer: It can hydrolyze proteins to form salts of amino acids.

Flashcard 99 — How many different amino acids are mentioned in the chapter as occurring in nature?

Answer: More than 20.

Flashcard 100 — Give two different structural forms of proteins.

Answer:

1. Fibrous form, such as keratin in hair and wool.
2. Approximately globular form, such as proteins found in eggs.

Flashcard 101 — Why do proteins have different biological functions?

Answer: Because their sizes, shapes, amino acid compositions, and amino acid sequences differ.

Flashcard 102 — Which of the following is NOT a characteristic of proteins?

a) They can contain carbon, oxygen, hydrogen, and sulfur.

b) They contain carbon, hydrogen, and nitrogen.

c) They react with acids and bases.

Answer: None of the options is necessarily excluded; this question is ambiguous as written in the uploaded chapter.

Flashcard 103 — Which units form sucrose?

a) Glucose

b) Fructose

c) Glucose and fructose

Answer: c) Glucose and fructose.

Flashcard 104 — Which element is not part of the general amino acid structure?

a) Nitrogen

b) Phosphorus

c) Oxygen

d) Carbon

Answer: b) Phosphorus.

Flashcard 105 — Which of the following substances are formed from fatty acids?

a) Proteins

b) Carbohydrates

c) Oils (lipids)

Answer: c) Oils (lipids).

Flashcard 106 — Justify: Fructose is considered a reducing sugar.

Answer: Because it gives positive reactions with Tollens' reagent and Fehling's solution.
